🧭 DM overview

Part 2 covers days five to ten: a flooded river, an orc toll gate, an ambush in the hills, and the arrival in Phandalin. It is written for 4–6 characters at level 1 or level 2, 2024 rules, and one 3-hour session or two shorter ones.

The travel-day routine, the crew and the DM toolkit from Part 1 all still apply. Place names other than Triboar, Conyberry and Phandalin are invented for this adventure.

The idea behind Part 2: the orcs are not villains. The dragon took their home and they are hungry. Players can fight them, pay them, trick them or help them. All four work.

🛤️ The adventure

🥾 Day 5: The white morning

Travel

The party leaves the shelter into a changed world and reaches the river by dusk. Nothing attacks today. The job of this day is to show what the storm did.

🌅 Recap. Open with Bessa's log from the end of Part 1, then ask a player to say in one sentence what their character remembers most about the storm.

🌅 Morning.

📖 Read aloud

The door rolls back and the light hurts. The plain is gone. In its place is a white sheet running to every horizon, smooth as a made bed, under a sky so blue it looks painted. It is completely silent. Then Odo steps out, goes in to his knees, and says a word he learned from Grett.

Bessa leaves a bag of nails in the niche, scratches one line in the visitors' book, and will not say what she wrote. It says: Snow. Door works.

🛤️ On the road.

📖 Read aloud

The trail is under a foot of snow, and you find it by the waystones and by Grett's memory. By midday the sun has turned the top to slush. By afternoon the wheels are cutting through to black mud, and every stream you cross is running fast and brown. All that snow is going somewhere.

If the party levelled up, ask each player to describe one thing their character can do now that they could not do five days ago.

👁️ Sighting: tracks. Use this if an ox bolted in Part 1.

📖 Read aloud

Hoofprints cross the trail, heading west at a run. An hour on, they are joined by other prints. Boots. Large ones. After that the hoofprints are walking, not running.

  • Wisdom (Survival) DC 10: three or four people, heavy, moving with purpose. They caught the ox and led it away.
  • Odo: "So Kevin's alive?" Grett: "For now. Depends how hungry they are."

If no ox bolted, the party finds only the boot prints, and a spot where someone stood for a long time watching the trail.

❓ One question. Ask a player: "Everything is quiet and bright after yesterday. Does your character trust it?"

🏕️ Camp.

📖 Read aloud

You hear the river an hour before you see it. When you do see it, Bessa stops the wagons without a word. It should be a ford and a timber bridge. It is sixty feet of brown water moving at the speed of a running horse, with whole trees turning over in it. The stone piers of the bridge still stand. The middle of the bridge is not there.

Beside the trail is the fifth waystone. It shows a hammer. Grett looks at the hammer, then at the bridge. "Mend what's broken. Thank you. Very helpful."

Camp is on the east bank. The night is quiet. Tell the players the river is louder in the morning.

🌊 Scene 6: The river

Puzzle

The caravan must cross a flooded river on an old dwarven ferry that can only make seven trips before the flood takes it. This is a planning puzzle. Put coins or tokens on the table for the loads and let the players push them around.

📖 Read aloud

Morning shows you what the dark hid. Just downstream of the broken bridge, a thick rope runs from a stone post on this bank to a stone post on the other. Tied up below it, half full of snowmelt, is a flat timber raft. On the waystone, under the hammer, is a carved panel: a raft, with two square blocks on it, and a row of small figures pulling a rope.

Grett reads the picture. "Ferry. For when the bridge is out. Dwarves build the spare before they build the thing." Then he looks at the water. "That rope's old. I wouldn't trust it past a handful of trips."

⛴️ How the ferry works

Explain this to the players in plain words before they start planning. Draw it if you can: two banks, two posts, one rope between them, one raft.

  • The guide rope runs from a stone post on the east bank to a stone post on the west bank. The raft hangs from it by an iron ring, so the current cannot carry the raft away.
  • The haul line is tied to the front of the raft. Someone standing on the west bank pulls it, and the raft slides across along the guide rope.
  • The river is too deep and too fast to pole or row. The only way to move a loaded raft is to pull it from the bank it is going to. A rope only pulls toward you.
  • A light return line lets anyone on the east bank drag the empty raft back. This is easy and needs no roll.

📜 The rules

Read these to the players, or hand them over on a card.

  1. The raft carries two loads per trip.
  2. An ox is one load. A wagon, empty or full, is one load. The bell is two loads and must come off its wagon to cross.
  3. People ride for free, as many as will fit.
  4. Every loaded trip must be pulled from the west bank. One ox on the west bank can pull any load with no roll. Two characters can pull instead, with a check.
  5. Bringing the empty raft back is free.
  6. The rope will last for seven loaded trips.

💪 Hauling by hand: one character makes a DC 12 Strength (Athletics) check, with Advantage if the second hauler describes how they help. On a failure the raft swings out and the trip takes twice as long. Mark one extra box.

❓ Questions players will ask

Question Answer
How do the first people get across to pull? Up to six characters can ride the empty raft and pull themselves hand over hand along the guide rope. It is slow, wet and safe. No roll, and it does not use a box.
Can we just swim across? A character can try. DC 12 Strength (Athletics), with Disadvantage in heavy armour and Advantage if tied to a safety line. On a failure they take 3 (1d6) bludgeoning damage and are swept back to the east bank. Riding the raft is easier.
Can we pull from the east side? Not as it is rigged. If a player suggests looping the haul line round the west post and back, like a pulley, say yes. Someone must cross to set it up. After that, oxen on the east bank can pull, and nobody needs to send oxen over first.
Can the oxen swim? One at a time, with a character alongside. See "Other ways across".
Can a wagon float? No. It would roll over and be gone in seconds. Grett is very sure about this.
Can we cross on the bridge piers? A character can jump the 15-foot gap with a running start if their Strength score is 15 or more. Otherwise it is a DC 12 Strength (Athletics) check, and a failure puts them in the water. Animals and wagons cannot.
Can we wait for the river to drop? It will take two days. That is four delay boxes, and Bessa will not do it.
Why only seven trips? The guide rope is old and is fraying where it rubs the post. Every loaded crossing saws at it.

⏲️ The flood clock

Draw seven boxes. Mark one for every loaded trip. When the seventh box is marked, read this:

📖 Read aloud

A whole pine tree comes down the river, roots first, turning slowly. It meets the rope. The rope hums, stretches, and parts with a crack like a whip. The raft is gone before the sound is.

Anything still on the east bank is left behind and comes off the tracker.

🧮 What it takes

A full caravan is 11 loads: the bell counts 2, three wagons count 3, six oxen count 6. That needs six trips, so there is one box to spare. A caravan that lost things in Part 1 has more room.

✅ One solution for a full caravan

Trip Loads Pulled by
1 Two oxen Two characters, who rode the empty raft over first
2 The bell An ox
3 Two wagons An ox
4 One wagon and one ox An ox
5 Two oxen An ox
6 One ox An ox

The key insight is to send oxen first, so that nobody has to haul by hand again.

🏊 Other ways across

Reward any of these. Players will invent more.

Idea How to run it
Swim an ox across A character swims or wades with it on a line. DC 13 Wisdom (Animal Handling). Success: the ox crosses and uses no raft space. Failure: it turns back and will not try again.
Mend the bridge The gap is 15 feet. Planks from one broken-up wagon will span it. DC 12 Strength check or any tool proficiency. People and oxen can then walk across. Wagons and the bell are too heavy. It costs one wagon and its pay. On a failed check it still works, but mark two boxes for the time lost.
Catch driftwood for planks DC 13 Strength (Athletics) to snag timber from the flood. Success: free planks for the bridge, with no wagon lost. Failure: 3 (1d6) bludgeoning damage and one box marked.
Leave something on purpose Always allowed. Update the tracker.
A spell or clever trick If it sounds plausible, it saves one trip.

💪 Falling in. A character who ends up in the water makes a DC 12 Strength (Athletics) check. Failure: 3 (1d6) bludgeoning damage, and they wash up 30 feet downstream on the same bank, cold and angry.

🗣️ Sample words

Moment Say something like
The first trip "The raft leaves the bank and the river takes hold of it at once. The rope goes tight as a bowstring. For a moment everything is leaning."
An ox on the raft "Gravy stands with her legs wide apart and her eyes shut. Odo tells her it's nearly over. He has been telling her that since the bank."
The bell crossing "It sits in the middle of the raft like a king being carried. The raft rides a hand's width lower. Halfway over, a wave slaps the side and the bell answers, one soft note across the water."
A failed haul "The raft swings downstream on the rope and hangs there, bucking. It takes everything you have to walk it back."
Box five marked "The water is over the lower step of the stone post now. It was dry this morning."
Box six marked "The rope is fraying where it rubs the post. You can see individual strands letting go."
Everything is across "Bessa counts wheels, then horns, then heads. She counts twice. Then she sits down on a wet rock, which you have never seen her do."

👥 Hints from the crew. Give one each time the players stall.

  • Odo: "Could the oxen pull it? They pull everything else."
  • Bessa: "The bell doesn't share. Send it alone and send it early, while the rope's good."
  • Grett: "Seven trips, I'd say. I'd bet on six. I wouldn't bet on eight."

After the crossing. Wet gear, a late start and a short afternoon's travel. The west bank climbs toward low brown hills.

⏳ Delay check. If six or more flood boxes were marked, mark one delay box. Mark one more for each wagon newly down to one ox.

🥾 Days 6 and 7: Into the hills

Travel

The plain ends and the hills begin. Across these two days the party learns it is being watched. Day 7 ends at the Notch.

🥾 Day 6, afternoon and night

🛤️ On the road.

📖 Read aloud

West of the river the land stops being flat. It rises in long ridges, like a rumpled blanket, and the trail no longer runs straight. It winds along the valley bottoms between them. The waist-high grass thins out into stony ground, gorse and bramble. Stands of scrub oak and dark pine grow on the slopes, and grey boulders the size of wagons lie about where some ancient ice left them.

On the plain you could see a rider ten miles off. Here you can see to the next bend in the trail or the next ridge, a few hundred paces at most. After six days of open sky, it feels like walking indoors.

What this changes for the players. Tell them plainly.

  • From the trail, they can see about 300 feet. Anything could be behind the next rise.
  • A scout who climbs a ridge can see for a mile, but can be seen from a mile away too.
  • The oxen are slower on the slopes, and the wagons cannot leave the trail at all now. On the plain that was a rule. Here it is a fact.
  • It is noisier: crows, wind in the pines, water running everywhere after the storm. It is harder to hear something coming.

👁️ Sighting: the cairn.

📖 Read aloud

On a rise beside the trail, someone has piled fresh stones into a cairn as tall as a person. On top is an ox skull, picked clean. It faces east, toward you.

  • Intelligence (History) or Wisdom (Survival) DC 10: an orc boundary marker. It means this land is claimed.
  • Intelligence (Investigation) DC 12: the skull is fresh, and it has a brand on the horn. Bessa knows the brand. "Harl Benn's team."

🏕️ Camp.

📖 Read aloud

Camp is in a hollow out of the wind. Everything you own is still damp from the river. Grett rigs lines and hangs blankets by the fire until the camp looks like a laundry. Nobody says so, but tonight both watches sit facing west.

The night is quiet. A watcher with passive Perception 13 or higher sees a single spark of firelight on a hilltop a mile off. It goes out when they look straight at it.

🥾 Day 7

🌅 Morning.

📖 Read aloud

You wake to the smell of somebody else's cooking fire, faint, on the west wind. Bessa has been up for an hour. "Trail log, day seven. We have neighbours."

🛤️ On the road.

📖 Read aloud

The hills close in. The white peak is hidden now behind nearer slopes, and somehow that is worse than seeing it. The trail climbs steadily. Twice you pass old dwarven stonework, a culvert and a retaining wall, still doing its job after centuries. The third time, the stonework has been pulled apart for building material, recently.

👁️ Sighting: the glint.

📖 Read aloud

High on the left-hand slope, something flashes in the sun. Then it does it again, slowly, three times.

  • Wisdom (Perception) DC 12: a figure lying flat on a rock, signalling with a piece of polished metal to someone further up the trail.
  • If a character climbs after it: 60 feet of loose rock and a DC 13 Strength (Athletics) check. The watcher is gone long before they arrive. It left a gnawed bone and a piece of slate with tally marks: three wagons, some oxen, and one large round shape with a question mark.

❓ One question. Ask a player: "You're being counted. How does your character feel about that?"

🛤️ The approach.

📖 Read aloud

By mid-afternoon the trail runs into a cleft between two shoulders of rock, so narrow the wagons will have to go through one at a time. The crew calls it the Notch. There used to be a dwarven arch here. There is now also a wall.

Begin Scene 7.

⚖️ Scene 7: The Notch

Choice

Orcs driven out of the mountains by the dragon have walled the trail and are charging a toll. The party can pay, talk, fight or go around, and each choice changes what happens in Scene 8.

📖 Read aloud

The wall is built of logs, dwarven masonry and the planks of at least two wagons. The gate is a wagon bed hung on ropes. Along the top stands a row of helmeted heads and shields, a dozen or more. Above them hangs a bedsheet painted with a tusked face. As you come within shouting distance, one figure stands up. It is very large. It is holding a slate.

"HALT. Welcome to Toll. Have goods ready for counting. Thank you for your... patience." It reads the last word off the slate.

👥 Who is here

Who What they are like
Brakk Orc toll-keeper. Huge, one broken tusk, and a pair of spectacle frames with no glass in them. He has discovered paperwork and he loves it. He is not cruel. He is hungry, and so is his tribe.
The orcs Three orcs for 4 players, four for 5, five for 6, and that count includes Brakk. Stats are in the overview under "Orc quick stats". Tired, thin, watching the sky as often as the trail.
The helmets Most of the heads on the wall are helmets on poles. Only the real orcs move.
Harl Benn A human merchant from Phandalin, chained by the ankle to a desk inside the gate. He taught Brakk bookkeeping to stay alive.
The pen Behind the wall: Harl's two surviving oxen, and any ox that bolted from the party in Part 1.

🏔️ Who the Broken Tusk are

This is for you. The players learn it from Brakk and from what they see.

  1. The tribe lived for generations in an old stronghold high in the mountains. They hunted elk and traded hides, and were no worse neighbours than anyone else.
  2. This autumn the white dragon took the stronghold in a single night. It killed those who fought and ignored those who ran.
  3. The survivors split. The angriest went to the storm-callers, a cult in the hills that worships a god of destruction and promised them revenge.
  4. Chief Gorza would not kneel to the cult. She led the rest down to the foothills: about sixty people, and more than half of them are children and elders.
  5. They have no herds and no stores, and winter is coming. The toll was Brakk's idea. It feeds people without getting them killed. Gorza's son Urzog thinks it is shameful, and he is halfway to the cult already.

Signs the party can see. Work one or two into your description.

  • Behind the Notch, the smoke of many small cooking fires, not one big one.
  • A child's face appears between two helmets on the wall and is pulled down by an unseen hand.
  • An old orc woman sits by the gate mending a net and does not look up.
  • The toll-keepers' armour hangs loose on them. They have all lost weight.

What Brakk says about it

  • "We are sixty. Twenty can fight. You do the sum."
  • "Storm-callers say: come, kneel, burn things. Chief says: burning things is not dinner."
  • "My daughter is behind this wall. She is six. She thinks the helmets are her uncles."
  • "Some of ours went to the storm. If you meet orcs with lightning painted on them, those are not us. Do not bring them receipt."

Brakk's quote: "No pay, no pass. Is rule. I did not make rule." A pause. "I did make rule. Is still rule."

🔍 What the party can notice

Check Result
Wisdom (Perception) DC 13 Most of the figures on the wall have not moved at all. There are only a handful of real orcs.
Wisdom (Insight) DC 10 Brakk keeps glancing at the sky. All of them do. They are afraid of something, and it is not the party.
Wisdom (Perception) DC 12, from near the gate Through the gaps: a pen with oxen in it, and a human at a desk, writing.
Bessa, no roll "That gate is Harl's wagon. I sold him that wagon."

🗣️ What Brakk will say

  • The toll is 20 gp, or two oxen, or one wagon of food.
  • The tribe is the Broken Tusk. They lived in the high country until "the Cold One" came and took it.
  • Chief Gorza told him to hold the trail and feed the tribe. Tolls feed more people than raids do. He worked that out himself and is proud of it.
  • Everyone who pays gets a receipt.

🔀 Four ways through

1. Pay. Take the toll off the tracker or out of the party's purses. Brakk writes a receipt with enormous care.

📖 Read aloud

He hands it down on the end of a spear. It says, in careful letters: 3 WAGON. 1 BELL. PAID. NO REFUND. Bessa reads it twice, folds it into her ledger, and says, in a voice you have not heard before, "That is a perfectly good receipt."

If the party was polite, Brakk gives the warning below.

2. Talk. Any of these can work.

Approach How to run it
Haggle Charisma (Persuasion) DC 13. Success halves the toll. Brakk respects haggling. "Clerk says this is normal."
Paperwork Charisma (Deception) DC 12 to present an official-looking exemption. Bessa's ledger, a seal or good handwriting gives Advantage. Brakk stamps it with a muddy thumb. No toll.
Threaten to ring the bell The orcs believe loud noise brings the Cold One. Charisma (Intimidation) DC 12 with Advantage. Success: the gate opens and no toll is paid, but Brakk gives no warning. If someone actually rings it, every orc dives for cover and the party has a minute to get through.
Tell them about the great herd No roll. If a player remembers the herd from day two and tells Brakk where it is, he goes very still. "How many?" This is the best outcome. See below.
Offer food Wagon 2's food counts as the toll, and the orcs fall on it before the gate is fully open.

🐃 The herd. Thousands of wild cattle, four days east, driven onto the plain by the same dragon. It is enough to feed the tribe through winter.

📖 Read aloud

Brakk takes off his spectacles. He says something over his shoulder in Orcish, and the wall goes quiet. Then the gate comes down, not up. Two orcs start pulling the helmets off the poles. "Toll is closed," Brakk says. "We go hunting." He looks at his slate for a moment, and then wipes it clean with his sleeve.

The Broken Tusk leave the trail. Brakk frees Harl Benn, returns the party's oxen and gives the warning below.

3. Fight. The orcs fight from the wall for one round, then come out, because they are hungry and impatient.

  • The wall is 10 feet high and gives cover to anyone on it. The gate has AC 13 and 15 hit points.
  • Brakk has 10 more hit points than the others. He surrenders when he drops below 10. "Toll is... waived."
  • The others give up or run when Brakk goes down, or when half of them have fallen. They are not fanatics.
  • Inside: the toll box with 45 gp, the pen, Harl Benn, and a slate map that marks a gully a day west with a drawing of round rocks.

4. Go around. A goat track climbs over the left shoulder.

  • Wisdom (Survival) DC 13 to get the wagons over.
  • Success: the party is past the Notch by dusk and unseen.
  • Failure: an axle cracks. They lose half a day, so mark one delay box, and a watcher sees them. In Scene 8 the orcs are fully ready.

🧾 Harl Benn

Harl Benn is a merchant from Phandalin, invented for this adventure. He left Triboar ten days ahead of the party with a load of roof slates, and the Broken Tusk took his wagon and oxen. The party has been finding his traces all along the trail: the empty wagon on day three, his brand on the ox skull on day six, and his wagon bed, which is now the Notch gate. He has stayed alive by keeping Brakk's accounts.

📖 Read aloud

The man at the desk is thin, ink-stained and astonishingly calm. "Bessa. You're late." "You're chained to a desk, Harl." "I've had worse postings. I would like it noted that I taught him addition, subtraction and receipts. I did not teach him interest. I'm not a monster."

  • If the party paid or talked: Brakk will release him for 10 gp, or a DC 13 Charisma (Persuasion) check. "Clerk is valuable. Clerk does sums."
  • If the party fought or told them about the herd: he is free.
  • Harl walks with the caravan to Phandalin. He pays the party 25 gp there, and he knows everyone in town.

🗣️ Brakk's warning

Give this if the party paid politely, talked well or told him about the herd.

📖 Read aloud

"Listen. Chief's son, Urzog. He does not like toll. He says toll is for cowards and clerks. He has his own band. One day west there is a gully. He waits on top with rocks." Brakk shrugs. "I would give you receipt for him. He cannot read."

🗣️ Sample lines for Brakk

  • On being insulted: "Rude. That is extra. I am writing 'rude'."
  • On being asked why he does this: "Before, we hunted. Now Cold One hunts where we hunted. Wagons are slower than elk. Is not complicated."
  • On the helmets being spotted: "They are... on break."
  • On the dragon: he will not say its name, and he lowers his voice. "It took the high hold in one night. It did not even want it. It wanted to sleep there."

⚔️ Day 8 and Scene 8: The gully

Fight

Urzog's raiders ambush the caravan in a narrow gully. How much the party knows in advance depends on Scene 7. This is the main fight of the adventure.

🌅 Morning.

📖 Read aloud

Day eight. The trail drops out of the Notch and winds between hills that crowd closer with every mile. Nobody talks much. Even Odo has noticed that the birds have stopped again.

🛤️ On the road.

📖 Read aloud

Around noon the trail enters a gully with steep, scrubby slopes on both sides and a strip of sky above. Fifty paces in, a fresh-cut tree lies across the wheel ruts. Its leaves are still green.

🔍 What the party knows

Situation Effect
Warned by Brakk, or found the slate map They know the place and the plan. Give them five minutes to make their own plan before entering. See "Turning the ambush" below.
Not warned Any character with passive Perception 13 or higher sees pebbles trickling down the slope and is not surprised. Everyone else is surprised in round one.
Seen going around the Notch Nobody gets the passive Perception check. The first boulder comes with no warning at all.

🪨 The ambush

📖 Read aloud

A voice from the top of the slope, young and very loud: "MY FATHER COUNTS. I TAKE!" Then a grinding noise, and a boulder the size of an ox comes over the edge.

⚔️ Enemies. Three orcs for 4 players, four for 5, five for 6, and that count includes Urzog. Stats are in the overview under "Orc quick stats". Urzog has 10 more hit points than the others and wears a single scale shed by the white dragon, the size of a dinner plate, on a cord round his neck. It is a trophy and not armour.

Urzog and the storm. Urzog is seventeen, and he watched the dragon take his home. A jagged lightning mark is painted, fresh and a little crooked, on his shield. The storm-callers told him that the strong take and the weak ask. His band is the tribe's young and angry. They are not the cult yet, but they are trying it on.

  • If captured, one of his band says: "The storm-callers do not count things. They do not hide behind helmets."
  • If Urzog is beaten and spared, he goes home to his mother, furious and alive. Brakk hears of it, and the Broken Tusk remember.
  • If he escapes, he goes to the cult. He can return later among the hostile orcs of the published adventure.

⚔️ Boulders. One rolls on each of the first rounds until they run out.

  • Each boulder is aimed at a wagon. Every creature within 5 feet of that wagon makes a DC 11 Dexterity saving throw, taking 7 (2d6) bludgeoning damage on a failure, or half as much on a success.
  • The oxen on that wagon panic. Until someone calms them with a DC 12 Wisdom (Animal Handling) check, that wagon cannot move.
  • A boulder does not destroy a wagon or the bell. If one hits wagon 1, the bell rings, deafeningly, and every orc flinches. They have Disadvantage on attacks until the end of the round.

🗺️ The slopes. Climbing up is difficult terrain. The top is 30 feet above the trail. Scrub gives cover to anyone who uses it.

What the orcs do

  1. Rounds one and two: roll boulders, throw javelins or shoot from the top.
  2. Round three: charge down the slope.
  3. Two of them ignore the party and go for the oxen, cutting harness to lead animals away. This is what they came for.
  4. They run when Urzog falls, or when half of them are down.

🧠 Turning the ambush

If the party was warned, let them plan. Good plans should work.

Plan How to run it
Sneak up the back of the slope Group Dexterity (Stealth) check, DC 12. If at least half succeed, the orcs are surprised and the party starts next to the boulders.
Use the boulders on the orcs A character at the top can lever one down the far side with a DC 12 Strength check. Orcs in its path make the same saving throw.
Send an empty wagon in as bait The orcs waste their boulders on it. Charisma (Deception) or a good description, no roll needed if it is clever.
Wait for night The orcs get bored and light a fire. They have Disadvantage on Perception.
Ring the bell on purpose Every orc flinches for one round, as above. It works once.
Talk Urzog does not negotiate. But if the Broken Tusk have gone hunting, one orc can be persuaded to tell him, with a DC 13 Charisma (Persuasion) check. Half his band leaves at once to join the hunt.

🍳 The crew in the fight

  • Bessa is under wagon 1 with the ledger, shouting which ox is in trouble.
  • Grett has a skillet and stands over the lead team. "Forty trips! Forty! And it's rocks!"
  • Odo has the ladle. Once in the fight, when a character is in real trouble, Odo hits an orc on the back of the head with it. It does no damage. The orc turns round in disbelief, and the character has Advantage on their next attack against it.

🗣️ Sample words for the fight

Moment Say something like
A boulder lands "It hits the trail, bounces once, and goes through the place you were standing. The ground shakes. The oxen scream."
The charge "They come down the slope in a slide of dust and stones, howling, and you realise how big they are."
An orc goes for the oxen "One of them is not looking at you at all. It has a knife out and it is sawing at Tuesday's harness."
A character drops an orc "It folds up and rolls the rest of the way down the slope. The ones behind it slow, just for a step."

The orcs break "They go up the slope faster than they came down it. In a few breaths the gully is quiet."

🩹 Aftermath

  • Loot: 35 gp in mixed coin, two Potions of Healing, and Urzog's trophy: one scale shed by the white dragon, the size of a dinner plate. It is not armour and has no magic. It is worth 25 gp, and more as proof that the dragon is real.
  • A captured orc will talk if treated fairly. The Cold One took the tribe's stronghold in the high peaks. Urzog found the scale near it and said it made him dragon-proof. It did not make him adventurer-proof.
  • Clearing the tree takes an hour. Update the tracker for any ox led away.
  • If Urzog lives, he remembers the party, and you have a recurring enemy if you want one.

⏳ Delay check. Mark one delay box for each wagon newly down to one ox, and one if the party waited for night.

🥾 Day 9: Conyberry

Travel

The last full day is quiet on purpose. It gives the players room to talk before the journey ends.

🌅 Morning.

📖 Read aloud

For the first time in days, you wake up and nothing is wrong. No frost. No smoke on the wind. Odo has made porridge and it is, by some accident, not burnt. He looks more worried about that than he did about the orcs.

🛤️ On the road.

📖 Read aloud

The hills open out. There are trees now, real ones, and a stream that behaves itself. In the afternoon the trail runs between low stone walls, then past a doorstep with no door, then a hearth with no house. A whole village, with the roofs gone and young birches growing in the rooms.

Grett takes off his cap as they pass through. "Conyberry. Raiders, years back. Before the lad was born." That is all he says.

🪨 The sixth waystone.

📖 Read aloud

In what was the village square stands a waystone. This one shows a beard. Odo looks at it for a long time. "Boot. Tankard. Flame. Hammer. Beard. What does a beard tell you to do?" Grett almost smiles. "Ask me tomorrow."

🏕️ Camp.

📖 Read aloud

The last camp is inside the walls of the old inn, where four fireplaces still stand and one still draws. Bessa says there is no need for the triangle tonight, and then puts the wagons in a triangle anyway.

❓ The last campfire. Take a little time here. Ask each player one of these:

  • "What will your character do first when you reach Phandalin?"
  • "Is there anything they'll miss about the trail?"
  • "Odo asks if you'd do it again. What do you tell him?"

Then Odo makes an announcement:

📖 Read aloud

"I've decided. I'm going to do the whole trip back as well." Grett puts down his bowl. "Lad. That was always the job. That's how carting works." A long silence. "Oh," says Odo.

🔔 Scene 9: Phandalin

Roleplay

The caravan arrives, the bell is rung, and the party is paid for what it kept alive. End on the job board.

🪨 The last stone.

📖 Read aloud

Mid-morning on the tenth day, the trail tops a rise. At the crest is one more waystone, carved with a mountain. Below it is a valley, and in the valley is a town: forty or fifty buildings of timber and fieldstone, built among the tumbled walls of something much older. There are no walls and no gate. Smoke rises from the chimneys.

Bessa stops the wagons. She looks at the town for a moment, and then at you. "Somebody ring it. They should know it's coming."

Ask the players who rings the bell. Let them decide. Then:

📖 Read aloud

The note rolls down the valley and comes back off the hills. A door opens. Then another. By the time the lead oxen reach the first house, half of Phandalin is standing in the road.

👥 Who they meet

Who Where How to play them
Harbin Wester, townmaster Behind the closed door of the townmaster's hall Nervous and pompous. Nobody has seen him in weeks. He speaks only through the door, and mostly to deny being in.
Toblen Stonehill Stonehill Inn Friendly and curious. He holds the town's purse for the bell and pays the party. The wine on wagon 3 is his order. Honours Pell's letter with a week of rooms. Without the letter, rooms are 5 sp a night.
Elmar Barthen Barthen's Provisions Kind and practical. He receives the supplies from wagon 2 and counts them twice.
Linene Graywind Lionshield Coster Sharp and fair. She will buy the white scale for 25 gp, or tell them to show it to Toblen first.
Mirna Behind the counter at Barthen's The baker's sister, if the party carried the letter from Triboar. She cries, then laughs. "The oven. She wrote all this way about the oven."

💰 Payday

Bessa takes the wagons straight to the Stonehill Inn. The townmaster ordered the bell, but the innkeeper holds the purse.

📖 Read aloud

The Stonehill Inn is a big, new-built roadhouse of fieldstone and rough timber. A broad man in an apron is already in the doorway, drying his hands and counting wagons with his eyes. "You're the bell." Then he sees wagon 3, and his whole face changes. "And that's my wine. Tell me that's my wine."

This is Toblen Stonehill. He pays from a strongbox under the bar. A note is pinned inside the lid, and he reads it aloud, a little embarrassed.

📖 Read aloud

"'Pay them what the manifest says and not a copper more. Deduct for lateness. Do not let them come to the house. H.W.'" He folds it up. "He's not a bad man. He's a man who saw a dragon and decided that indoors was a career."

If the wine was lost, Toblen looks at the empty wagon for a long moment. "I'll tell the regulars it's a dry season."

If the party knocks on the townmaster's door anyway:

📖 Read aloud

A voice from inside: "The townmaster is not at home." "Then who is speaking?" A long pause. "His door."

Go down the tracker card out loud, line by line. This is the players' score.

Delivered Pay
The bell 50 gp
Each wagon of goods 20 gp
Each ox 5 gp
Harl Benn, alive 25 gp, from Harl himself
All three crew arrive uninjured 5 gp to each character from Bessa's own purse

Then take off 5 gp for every delay box marked. Toblen is apologetic about it. "Townmaster's terms. I only hold the box." The total cannot go below zero.

If the party shows Toblen the white scale, the common room goes quiet. He carries it to the townmaster's house himself, and comes back with 10 gp and a pale face. "He says read the board. He says please."

➕ Hanging the bell (optional)

Use this in the long version. It mirrors loading the bell in Triboar.

📖 Read aloud

The Shrine of Luck is a small building of piled stones, and its new bell-frame was built by someone who had the measurements nearly right. Half the town has come to watch. The other half has come to give advice.

  • Three jobs at DC 12: Strength (Athletics) on the rope, Dexterity (Acrobatics) to guide it from the roof, and Charisma (Persuasion) to stop the crowd helping.
  • All succeed: it goes up in one clean pull, and the town cheers.
  • Any fail: it goes up anyway, slightly crooked, and stays that way for good. People will give directions by it. "Left at the crooked bell."

🌇 The evening

📖 Read aloud

At sunset someone rings it properly for the first time. The sound goes out over the roofs and up the valley and into the hills. In the common room of the Stonehill Inn, conversations stop, and then start again a little louder.

Grett finishes the waystones.

📖 Read aloud

"Mountain. That's the last one." He counts on thick fingers. "Boot, tankard, flame, hammer, beard, mountain. Walk. Drink. Get warm. Mend what's broken. Grow old." He nods at the last. "Come home. That's the whole trail. Dwarves like instructions."

Bessa closes the book.

📖 Read aloud

"Trail log, day ten. Cargo delivered. Guards..." She stops, crosses something out, and writes again. "Guards recommended." She tears out the page and gives it to you. "I go east in three days. If you ever want the walk, you know where the trail is."

📌 The job board

📖 Read aloud

Outside the townmaster's hall, on a board under a little roof, three notices are pinned. All three are in the same cramped handwriting, and all three offer gold. In the last of the light, far to the north-east, something pale turns once above the white peak and is gone.

End the session here. The three notices are the starting quests of Dragon of Icespire Peak.

🤝 Hand-off to Dragon of Icespire Peak

The party arrives with a reason to stay, people who know them, and first-hand knowledge of the threat. Start the next session at the job board.

⬆️ Level. The published adventure assumes level 1 characters at the job board. If yours are level 2, the first quests will be a little easier, which suits a new table. If you kept them at level 1, they match the book exactly, and you can award level 2 for delivering the bell. Either way, follow the book's rules for levelling from there.

🤫 The quiet mile

This is a second "one action each" scene, like the storm in Part 1. There is no fight.

📖 Read aloud

Odo sees it first, and for once he says nothing at all. He just points. On a ridge to the south, perhaps a mile away, something white is crouched over something red. It is the size of a house. It has not noticed you. The trail runs along the open hillside in full view of it for the next mile. And on wagon 1, with every turn of the wheels, the bell is swinging.

Each player gets one job. The DC is 12.

Job Typical check
Muffle the bell with blankets and lash down the striker beam Dexterity (Sleight of Hand) or Strength
Keep the oxen calm and quiet Wisdom (Animal Handling)
Grease the axles so the wheels stop squealing Any tool proficiency, or Intelligence
Keep Odo from saying anything Charisma (Persuasion or Intimidation)
Pick the line of travel that uses the dips in the ground Wisdom (Survival)
Watch the dragon and call when to stop and when to move Wisdom (Perception)
Failures Result
None or one The caravan creeps past. The dragon never looks up. Everyone breathes out at once.
Two It lifts its head and looks straight at you for a long moment. Then it goes back to eating. Each character makes a DC 10 Wisdom saving throw or has the Frightened condition until the caravan is out of sight.
Three or more It takes off, passes over low enough to frost the canvas, picks one ox out of its harness without landing, and is gone. Remove one ox. Nobody is hurt. Nobody speaks for an hour.

The pedant
